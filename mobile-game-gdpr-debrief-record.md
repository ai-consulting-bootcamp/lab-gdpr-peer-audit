# GDPR peer-audit comparison — written review

**Reviewer** Sean Langsdorf · **Builder whose published self-audit was reviewed** Jędrzej Czarnota

**How this comparison was done.** I completed the independent report before opening Jędrzej's [published GDPR self-audit](https://github.com/jedrzejczarnota-source/lab-gdpr-self-audit/blob/eff19e436f8a6a7f7b019a2ab846a8e4c1355d9c/02_gdpr_first_pass_audit.md). The comparison below uses that document in place of a live exchange. No meeting, builder response to my report, reciprocal review of CompanyScope or jointly approved closing note is claimed. The independent report remains unchanged.

| Debrief step | What the written comparison establishes |
| --- | --- |
| 1 · Auditor presents | The [independent report](mobile-game-gdpr-peer-audit-report.md) recommends holding live use of the reviewed hosted configuration. The offline check found a fixed custom namespace and a fallback email destination. These are configuration risks, not confirmed incidents. |
| 2 · Builder responds | No live response. Jędrzej's written self-audit describes the intended flow and its existing caveats, including the lack of pre-send human review, undocumented vendor assurances and the limits of clearing a namespace. I treat this as the builder's prior position, not a reply to my findings. |
| 3 · Compare lawful bases | Both assessments see a possible legitimate-interest basis for narrow public-source research, subject to necessity, balancing and safeguards. The builder tentatively considers Art. 6(1)(b) for delivery and optional PDF personalization. I agree that necessary delivery may fit where the requester is party to the contract, but the studio's contract does not automatically cover an employee requester or third parties named in uploaded documents. Whether the feature is optional does not resolve whose data is necessary for whose contract. For live PDFs, my position remains that the basis and necessity are unproven, and redacted business-only inputs should be the starting point. |
| 4 · Compare DPIA conclusions | Both assessments favor a DPIA before the unrestricted hosted workflow processes real PDFs that may contain personal data. The builder emphasizes combining sources and innovative technology. I would not infer matching about individuals merely because company and PDF data meet in one workflow. My conclusion relies on the combination of unrestricted inputs, potential cross-client access, external synthesis and automatic delivery. A narrower, segregated deployment should be screened separately using actual scale and data. |
| 5 · Compare gap lists | Both identify the need for processor terms, transfer evidence, retention, rights handling and stronger isolation. The builder already identified the cross-tenant risk of clearing a shared namespace and the lack of selective erasure. The independent review traced the same static scope from write through retrieval and separately identified the fixed mailbox fallback, which I did not find addressed in the self-audit. The builder went further on the formal records of processing, incident response and law stacking. On release timing, the builder's memo says “proceed with conditions” and lists per-client namespaces before scaling, while also requiring a synthetic deletion test before live personal data. I would make proven isolation and verified delivery explicit requirements before the first hosted live run. |
| 6 · Closing reflection | Individual reflection below. It has not been reviewed or approved by both participants. |

## Individual closing reflection

The hardest gaps to spot in your own workflow are often defaults that work during a one-user demo, such as a shared storage scope or an email fallback. Reading the self-audit showed that the builder had already spotted important retention and deletion issues. The independent review added value by following where a document could be retrieved and where a report could be sent, then turning both findings into controls to test before any live run.
